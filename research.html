Interests & Publications

My research interests include optimal stopping and free boundary problems, optimal control, problems with partial information and filtering techniques. The studied problems are motivated by financial and actuarial applications.

Publications

Filtering in a hazard rate change-point model and financial applications

M. Buttarazzi, C. Ceci · 2026 · International Journal of Theoretical and Applied Finance

Journal article ArXiv
Abstract ▾
This paper develops a continuous-time filtering framework for estimating a hazard rate subject to an unobservable change-point. This framework naturally arises in both financial and insurance applications, where the default intensity of a firm or the mortality rate of an individual may experience a sudden jump at an unobservable time, representing, for instance, a shift in the firm's risk profile or a deterioration in an individual's health status. By employing a progressive enlargement of filtration, we integrate noisy observations of the hazard rate with default-related information. We characterise the filter, i.e. the conditional probability of the change-point given the information flow, as the unique strong solution to a stochastic differential equation driven by the innovation process enriched with the discontinuous component. A sensitivity analysis and a comparison of the filter's behaviour under various information structures are provided. Our framework further allows for the derivation of an explicit formula for the survival probability conditional on partial information. This result applies to the pricing of credit-sensitive financial instruments such as defaultable bonds, credit default swaps, and life insurance contracts. Finally, a numerical analysis illustrates how partial information leads to delayed adjustments in the estimation of the hazard rate and consequently to mispricing of credit-sensitive instruments when compared to a full-information setting.

The market value of optimal annuitization and bequest motives

M. Buttarazzi, G. Stabile · 2024 · Mathematical and Statistical Methods for Actuarial Sciences and Finance, Springer

Journal article
Abstract ▾
Since the seminal contribution of Yaari (1965), who showed that individuals with no bequest motive should convert all their retirement wealth into annuities, a number of papers have analysed the annuitization decision under the so-called all or nothing institutional arrangement, where immediate lifetime annuities are purchased just at a one point in time. In this paper, we investigate the effect of linear bequest motives on the annuitization decision for a retired individual who maximizes the market value of future cash flows. Finally, we present numerical examples analyzing optimal annuitization under strong or weak bequest motives.

Optimal Annuitization with stochastic mortality: Piecewise Deterministic Mortality Force

M. Buttarazzi, T. De Angelis, G. Stabile · 2025 · Submitted

ArXiv
Abstract ▾
We examine the lifetime asset allocation and annuitization problem for a retiree under a stochastic mortality framework, where the hazard rate of death follows a piecewise deterministic Markov process (PDMP). The retiree seeks to maximize the expected discounted utility from consumption, bequest, and remaining wealth upon complete conversion of assets into annuities. We formulate the problem as a stochastic control problem with optimal stopping, deriving the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) variational inequality and providing a complete verification theorem. Numerical experiments illustrate how sudden changes in health status drastically alter the optimal timing of annuitization.

Pricing Options in a Duopoly Game with Asymmetric Information

M. Buttarazzi · 2024 · Submitted

ArXiv
Abstract ▾
We analyze an American-style duopoly option game where two competing firms hold the option to enter an emerging market under asymmetric information regarding market demand. One firm has superior informational flow modeled as a filtered probability space with a progressive enlargement. We characterize the Bayesian-Nash equilibrium of the game through a system of coupled free-boundary problems. The resulting preemption and attrition equilibria show that information advantages create strategic delays, altering the threshold boundary at which exercise occurs.

PhD Thesis

Mathematical models for optimal annuitization

Matteo Buttarazzi · 2025 · Sapienza University of Rome

PDF
Abstract ▾
Purchasing an annuity is one of the most significant financial choices individuals can make for retirement. Lifetime annuities provide guaranteed income until death, thereby eliminating longevity risk. Despite theoretical models predicting complete annuitization under certain conditions, empirical evidence reveals that individuals rarely annuitize their entire wealth—a phenomenon known as the annuity puzzle. This thesis investigates mathematical models for optimal annuitization to better understand decision-making under uncertainty, mortality risk, and bequest motives.
