Research Interests

  • Optimal Stopping, Free-boundary Problems, Insurance and Mathematical Finance, Partial Information and Filtering.

Research Overview

A central problem in my research is to determine the optimal time (if any) at which an individual, investing in a financial fund, should irreversibly convert their wealth into a lifetime annuity providing a guaranteed income stream.

In [2], under the assumption of a constant mortality force, the problem is formulated as a one-dimensional optimal stopping problem for a geometric Brownian motion describing the evolution of the financial fund. This setting allows for explicit solutions and a complete characterization of the optimal strategy.

This framework is extended in [3] by introducing stochastic mortality modelled as a piecewise deterministic Markov process, with random jump sizes and exponentially distributed interarrival times. The resulting problem is initially three-dimensional, as it includes financial wealth, the number of health shocks, and the current mortality state. However, it can be reduced to a sequence of nested one-dimensional optimal stopping problems. We identify the possible geometries of the continuation and stopping regions, establish criteria to distinguish among them, and characterize the value function as the solution to a free boundary problem.

In [4], applying the framework developed in [3], we study the impact of a single irreversible health shock with deterministic jump size. We derive explicit analytical expressions for both the value function and the optimal stopping boundaries.

The above works assume full information on the underlying processes. In [1], we relax this assumption by considering a model in which the stochastic intensity of a random time switches between two states at an unobservable change-point. The exact time of the switch is unobserved, and one has only access to noisy observations of the intensity. Using techniques from stochastic filtering and progressive enlargement of filtrations, we characterize the conditional distribution of the change-point as the unique strong solution of a stochastic differential equation driven by an innovation process with jumps.

An ongoing project investigates optimal annuitization under partially observable mortality, where individuals receive noisy signals about their health status. The problem is driven by two interacting diffusions, describing the dynamics of wealth and the evolution of beliefs, leading to a challenging optimal stopping problem under partial information.
